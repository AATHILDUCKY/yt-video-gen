# Relevant subscribe line and musical finish

Finish the explanation before asking for a subscription. Resolve the opening question, state the useful takeaway, then deliver one short, natural sentence about the value of future videos. Match the CTA to the topic; do not promise a follow-up investigation, upload schedule or guaranteed protection that the channel has not committed to.

Suggested lines:

- Science/physics learning: “For more clear explanations, subscribe to this channel.”

- General tech/news: “For the latest tech news and cybersecurity updates, subscribe to this channel.”
- Cybersecurity: “For more cybersecurity updates and clear breakdowns of digital threats, subscribe to this channel.”
- Practical security: “For more clear explanations of how to protect your digital life, subscribe to this channel.”
- Technology explainers: “Subscribe for more clear explanations of the technology shaping your world.”
- After a breach/update story: “For more on what tech and security updates mean for you, subscribe to this channel.”

These are editorial options, not scientifically proven highest-converting scripts. Choose or lightly rewrite one relevant sentence rather than stacking them. Keep the same narrator, delivery and normal speech speed. Do not add a list of requests to like, comment, share and subscribe, or a long goodbye. Avoid implying that a subscription itself makes viewers secure.

## Music

Move into the story's resolution arrangement under the takeaway and carry it through the CTA. Keep the same motif, compatible harmony and sonic character. For a sensitive breach or unresolved investigation, choose a restrained resolved phrase rather than a celebratory jingle. For an upbeat product explanation, a gentle positive lift may fit. Do not abruptly swap to an unrelated stock track.

Duck music beneath the closing sentence. After the final word, allow only the short musical landing/tail that fits the runtime, often roughly half to one second; measure rather than imposing that estimate. A modest lift after speech can help the ending land, but avoid sudden loudness jumps. Fade or resolve naturally without cutting the reverb or narrator.
