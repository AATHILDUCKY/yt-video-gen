# Modern educational animation

Use this direction for the user's default modern Duckygen look. Keep an existing project's approved visual identity during scoped edits. A specifically requested Lemo style or supplied brand can override the default look; preserve factual accuracy, legibility and measured narration.

## Choose a coherent treatment

Default to modern editorial science: warm paper, charcoal, selective coral, purposeful green, clear vector geometry, matte spatial depth, restrained texture, large labels and clean typography. Keep the sketch character in deliberate contours or occasional explanatory marks; avoid making every object a rough doodle. Manrope or Inter works for narration labels; Barlow Condensed for short emphasis; IBM Plex Mono for measured values. Treat one equation as a visual object with aligned symbols and sufficient space.

Use Lemo's Swiss Motion for hierarchy and alignment, Isometric Infographic for spatial flow, Exploded Teardown for hidden layers, Engineering Briefing Board for measured mechanisms, and Data Storytelling for quantities. Whiteboard/Chalkboard can supply progressive construction without copying a classroom look. Choose one primary grammar and borrow a compatible technique when useful; do not cycle styles between chapters. Search the bundled catalog before reading selected STYLE.md files.

Compose scenes around the mechanism: large system views, purposeful close-ups, comparisons, changing geometry and measured traces. Vary the actual arrangement and scale; repeated centered headings above tiny static diagrams look like slides. Use occlusion, contact shadows and material differentiation when they clarify depth. Keep the most important action visually dominant. Small details and texture should survive phone-size review without competing with the subject.

## Choreograph the idea

Establish an object, reveal its connection, move the quantity, show contact or arrival, then show the receiver's changed state. Attach effects to the same anchors and clock as the mechanism. Build graphs from their real data. Use matched objects, shape-preserving transforms, wipes through geometry or purposeful cuts for continuity. A continuous camera move must reveal something; it is not a substitute for explanation.

Use smooth eases or a small damped settle for labels, highlights and editorial transitions. Physical trajectories, force magnitudes, ray angles and clocks come from the model. Never apply a decorative ease to gravity, orbital speed or a plotted measurement. Character exaggeration may support a metaphor but cannot serve as quantitative evidence.

Let important type finish entering before the viewer needs to read it. Move one main focus at a time. Keep measurement labels steady during observation; stage a camera reframe before the calculation. Reserve emphasis for a consequence or answer. Avoid arbitrary shake, particles, continuous pulsing, RGB splits, generic glowing HUDs and gratuitous zooms.

## Prove the look

Render representative setup, mechanism and answer frames with the actual drawing code. Inspect the mechanism in motion and compare it at phone scale. Check composition, contrast, silhouette, label overlap, arrow attachment, accurate dimensions/axes and the causal event. Reuse unchanged reviewed assets and frames. A palette, adjective such as “premium,” or imported style prompt is not a visual-quality test.

For temporal motion blur, keep captions and changing numerical readouts sharp: composite them after the motion samples, or hold their values at the output frame’s center while physical geometry samples the requested subframe times. A rapidly changing time label averaged across subframes produces overlapping digits even when the trajectory is correct.
